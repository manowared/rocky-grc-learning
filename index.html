Module 1 · Foundations

SOX, ICFR, and why controls matter

Learning goal: Explain the purpose of internal control over financial reporting (ICFR), distinguish management's role from an external auditor's role, and recognize what makes a control testable.

1. What is SOX?

The Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002 introduced reforms intended to strengthen corporate accountability and financial reporting. Section 404 addresses management's assessment of internal control over financial reporting and, for applicable issuers, an independent auditor's attestation.

2. What is ICFR?

ICFR is a process designed to provide reasonable assurance about the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes. Controls help reduce the risk that material errors or fraud will go undetected or unprevented.

Think in cause and effect: risk → control objective → control activity → evidence → evaluation. A policy existing on paper does not, by itself, prove a control operated.

3. Who does what?

Management owns and maintains the company's controls and is responsible for its ICFR assessment. External auditors independently audit ICFR when required and express an opinion under the applicable auditing standards. A technology risk analyst may support management by documenting risks, coordinating evidence, and tracking exceptions, depending on their role.

4. Design versus operation

Design: if performed as intended by a competent person, could the control prevent or detect the relevant error or fraud in time? Operating effectiveness: did the control actually operate as designed, by the right person, with appropriate consistency and evidence?

Common trap: “The company has a policy” is not enough to conclude the control is effective. You need to understand the actual control activity and evaluate appropriate evidence.

Case 001 · Access management

Investigate the terminated employee

Apex Software uses a financial application to process customer transactions and record revenue. HR records employee departures; IT manages system access. A preliminary review raises a question about whether terminated employees' access is removed promptly.

Your task: inspect the fictional evidence below before deciding what to do next. Open each evidence item; do not assume that a single record proves the whole control worked.

Evidence A · HR termination record

Employee: Jordan Lee · Termination effective: 14 Oct · Status: Confirmed

HR record HR-1042 shows Jordan Lee's employment ended on 14 October. It does not show whether application access was disabled.

Evidence B · Access log

System: Apex Finance · Account: jlee · Last login: 16 Oct

The fictional application log records a successful login for account jlee on 16 October, two days after the termination effective date. Confirm identity mapping and timestamps before drawing a conclusion.

Evidence C · IT ticket

Ticket IT-8821 · Request: disable account · Status: Closed

Ticket IT-8821 was created on 14 October and marked closed on 17 October. The ticket has no attached screenshot or system-generated disablement log in the supplied case file.

Decision 1: What is the strongest next step?

Choose the most defensible action based on the evidence available so far.

Decision 2: What can you conclude?

After reviewing the supplied records, choose the most accurate conclusion.

This simulated evidence is intentionally incomplete. A responsible reviewer distinguishes an observed fact, a potential exception, and a final conclusion supported by sufficient appropriate evidence.

Illustrative reference solution

Risk: A terminated employee may retain access to a financial application and make unauthorized transactions or changes.

Potential impact: Unauthorized activity could affect the validity, accuracy, or completeness of revenue or related financial information; actual impact depends on permissions and activity.

Example control: HR sends timely termination notifications to a designated IT access administrator, who disables relevant access within a defined timeframe. A reviewer periodically reconciles termination records to access-removal records, investigates exceptions, and documents resolution.

Evidence: HR termination report, application account status or disablement audit log, timestamps, reviewer sign-off, and exception resolution records. A ticket marked closed alone may not prove the precise action or timing.

Testing approach: Define the population and period, select a justified sample, trace termination records to system-generated access-removal evidence, compare timing against the documented requirement, and investigate exceptions. The scope depends on the actual control and methodology.
