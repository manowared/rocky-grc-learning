Workpaper 001 · Risk assessment

Terminated-user access

Business scenario

Apex Software uses a financial application to process customer transactions and record revenue. Human Resources maintains employee termination records. IT administers application access.

During a preliminary review, the technology risk team learns that the company does not have a documented, consistently followed process for confirming that terminated employees lose access to the financial application.

This is fictional scenario information. No actual company or real control failure is being described.

Review criteria

Use these criteria to review your work. They are a learning checklist, not an automated audit opinion.

  • Risk: Does your answer identify unauthorized access after termination?
  • Reporting impact: Does it explain how unauthorized transactions or changes could affect financial records or reported amounts?
  • Control design: Does the control identify a responsible owner, a trigger or frequency, and the required access-removal action?
  • Evidence: Does it identify records that could demonstrate the process occurred, such as termination notifications and access-removal logs?
  • Precision: Does the proposed process help prevent or detect the relevant risk in a timely manner?

A control's suitability depends on the company's actual systems, risks, other controls, and financial-reporting scope.

Reference solution

Illustrative example for learning. It is not the only acceptable answer or a substitute for a company-specific risk assessment.

Risk

A terminated employee may retain access to the financial application and use it to make unauthorized transactions or changes to financial data.

Potential financial-reporting impact

Unauthorized transactions or changes could affect the accuracy, completeness, or validity of recorded revenue or related financial information. The actual impact depends on the access granted and the transactions affected.

Example management control

HR sends termination notifications to an assigned IT access administrator. IT disables the individual's relevant application access according to a defined timeframe. A designated reviewer periodically compares termination records with access-removal records, investigates exceptions, and documents resolution.

Potential evidence

  • Employee termination report or approved notification.
  • Application account-disablement record or audit log.
  • Evidence of the review and any exception follow-up.

What would testing involve?

A reviewer could inspect a defined sample of termination records, trace each item to access-removal evidence, compare dates against the company's documented requirement, and investigate exceptions. The actual test scope and sample would depend on the control and the applicable methodology.

Important distinction

Documenting a control does not prove it operated effectively. Design assessment and operating-effectiveness testing require appropriate evidence. Rocky's example does not constitute a real audit conclusion.
